## Research Findings

Emperor Constantine the Great's reign, spanning from 306 to 337 CE, represents a pivotal epoch that profoundly shaped the trajectory of Europe. His decisions initiated a comprehensive transformation encompassing the continent's religious landscape, geopolitical configuration, and the fundamental relationship between secular and spiritual authority. By actively promoting Christianity beyond mere toleration through the Edict of Milan (313 CE), Constantine integrated the Church into the state apparatus, providing it with privileges and funding, which dramatically accelerated its growth and established it as Europe's dominant faith. This move not only ended centuries of persecution but also imbued European civilization with a unifying Christian identity that would define its culture, laws, and moral framework for millennia.

A cornerstone of Constantine's impact was the founding of Constantinople in 330 CE. This strategic relocation of the imperial capital eastward not only shifted the geopolitical heart of the Roman Empire but also contributed significantly to the eventual schism between the Western and Eastern Roman Empires. Constantinople emerged as a vibrant hub of culture, economy, and religion, preserving classical learning and Roman legal traditions, thereby profoundly influencing the development of Southeastern Europe and the Near East while the West underwent fragmentation.
